# Bridge actor GPU migration: isolated geometry prototype

This is a preparatory slice, not a production migration. The live renderer still paints and uploads the complete actor Canvas, preserving authored fighter art, effects, lighting and draw order. There is no claimed performance improvement. An opt-in diagnostic overlay is wired to the live WebGL presentation; it does not replace Canvas actors. It has been smoke-tested in software Chromium, not on a physical GPU.

`GPUActorBatch` emits bounded camera-relative segments from the live solved skeleton (including hip sockets, waist, neck, toes and elbows), with approximate player and enemy limb widths and optional diagnostic weapon segments. Its buffer is independent of the fixed SACR v4 shadow/reflection UBO. A batch with overflow or non-finite coordinates must be discarded as a whole, not partially presented. The shaders demonstrate instanced tapered capsule coverage and accept those records; they do not implement authored body and weapon rendering. Enemy weapon proxies follow their painted attachment, angle and nominal lengths, including shield hand and perched aim; their capsule shapes omit material, detail and telegraphs. The player scabbard follows the same computed geometry as the Canvas painter, and the diagnostic blade follows its projected grip and length when fully drawn. The diagnostic omits the blade during draw/sheathe transitions rather than pretending to reproduce Canvas clipping. Neither is production weapon art. Segment overlap, style, lighting, material, articulation details, combat telegraphs and layering remain unsolved.

Before switching any production actor to GPU, the next integration needs an explicit compositing plan for enemies, silhouettes, strike smears, player, projectiles, foreground and effects; faithful player/enemy geometry and weapons; equivalent receiver lighting, flashes and motion effects; bounded resource lifecycle and context-loss fallback; visual comparisons across combat states; and hardware GPU traces. The existing Canvas must remain authoritative until those checks pass. No change to SACR layout or gameplay is made here.

## Live diagnostic overlay

Add `?bridgeActorDebug=1` to the page before loading. On an admitted Bridge WebGL path this compiles an extra program, allocates one bounded instance buffer, and overlays translucent cyan player and amber enemy proxies after the final grade. It is a geometry alignment diagnostic, not a production rendering mode: it deliberately ignores occlusion, actor finish and layer order. Canvas and SACR remain authoritative, the overlay is not drawn during staged opening or on Canvas fallback, and an invalid or overflowed packet is skipped entirely. The debug overlay's draw count and overflow state appear in the GPU presentation receipt. No extra shader, buffer or draw is installed when the flag is absent.

The software Chromium fixture `.qa/bridge-actor-prototype.mjs` ran against the production build with a compact backing, once on Master and once on Optimized; both submitted 28 instances without GL errors and showed changed pixels when the overlay was disabled. This is a smoke test only: it does not establish visual parity, performance, physical-device support, or correct foreground ordering.

## Anatomical input refinement

The diagnostic packet now uses the actual rear/front femur sockets and waist rather than the shadow outline, with separate approximate enemy stroke widths and player humanoid proportions. Enemies contribute weapon-specific diagnostic primitives rather than their SACR cast segment, so the shield follows the off-hand, the hammer has an approximate head, and a perched gunner follows its actual aim. Painter outlines, player curved contours, depth, scabbard transitions, material shading, hit flashes and VFX remain Canvas-only; capsule overlap and render order are not fidelity-correct. This work is still behind the diagnostic flag and makes no change to combat or SACR.

The shared scabbard calculation reuses a scratch object; the live Canvas renderer remains authoritative. Transition blade geometry, fittings, rear-carry ordering, animation overlays, face/hand contours, and material details are not yet implemented on GPU.
