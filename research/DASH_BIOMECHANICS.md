# Grounded dash: biomechanics and implementation notes

Research and implementation reference, 2026-11-09. Stickman Rising is a stylized action game; its pixels, forces, timings and drag coefficients are not calibrated to real-world units or athletic measurements.

## What informed the design

- Sprint acceleration emphasizes directing ground-reaction force backward and downward so the runner accelerates forward. A whole-body forward inclination, coordinated hip extension and shin angle are more useful cues than folding at the waist. As a runner reaches sustained top speed, posture normally rises; our short anime burst deliberately maintains more lean for readability. [Sprint mechanics](https://thesprint.club/sprinting-simplified/)
- Braking is a different action from acceleration: the foot meets the ground and the hips, knees and ankle absorb momentum. The torso can become more upright relative to the lower body, and the center of mass can lower. That supports a compressed grounded catch and a controlled rise, rather than a rigid return to idle. [Deceleration in sport](https://www.sportsinjurybulletin.com/improve/demands-of-sport-part-v--braking-new-ground) and [running gait phases](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-7078/5/2/40)
- Still-air resistance grows approximately with the square of velocity in the applicable regime. The effective frontal area of a moving runner varies with running mechanics; it is not eliminated by leaning. Forward lean during acceleration is also about applying force, not merely avoiding air resistance. [Measured air resistance during walking and running](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10716652/)

## Game interpretation

- A grounded dash press begins a planted charge. Early release starts a regular dash; holding to full and releasing triggers a finite, stronger power dash. Its powered drive has a small visual foot clearance above the floor while the collision and movement motor remain grounded; it catches the floor again before recovery. A grounded stabilization and rise follows a power dash; attacks remain available, and a held next charge waits until stabilization ends. Air/vertical dashes and normal attack-to-dash cancels remain immediate.
- The dash motor applies a velocity-squared resistance; ordinary player movement also receives a small horizontal air-drag term. Lean modestly changes the effective coefficient during forward ground drive. Ground/air damping and braking remain separate arcade-tuned effects. There is no wind simulation or claim of physically accurate aerodynamics.
- The normal grounded acting layer stays upright through the push and gather, settling into an upright, low split-stance catch with the arms trailing before rising. The charged dash alone uses the near-horizontal drive. The hips and knees participate, and foot IK supports the normal dash and catch; the power drive briefly keeps both feet just above the floor. Catch compression lowers the rendered center of mass without changing the collision ground plane.
- The charged dash uses its own authored pose clip, near-horizontal drive, low catch and a separate stabilization/rise; it carries stronger momentum with limited steering while settling. Charge tremor, bounded ground-relative wake accents, a launch crack and directional stone fragments emphasize the force. Existing shake settings continue to control camera shake; no new full-screen flash is required. Effects follow actual movement, so a reversal does not falsely burst in the old direction.
- The dash pose also participates during a dash sword strike, while the sword's attack animation and actual hit resolution remain active. See the automated tests for frame rates, contact, responsiveness, combat, air drag and wall behavior.

## Research follow-up: acceleration, braking, and animation timing

- A high-speed-video study of eight world-class male sprinters associates first-ground-contact performance with a forward-leaning trunk and thigh separation at take-off. This is a visual guide, not a claim that the game's exaggerated pose or forces recreate a sprint start. [Kinematic factors associated with start performance](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0021929021003341)
- A braking coaching overview describes lowering through the joints, placing the lead foot ahead of the body, and keeping the trunk upright or slightly reclined; exaggerated overreaching is not necessary. This informs the low front-foot catch and upright torso in the sketch. [Deceleration and braking mechanics](https://www.evercourts.com/resources/deceleration-and-braking-mechanics-for-racquet-sports-players)
- Animation principles emphasize readable anticipation, staggered follow-through, and balancing timing with responsiveness. The player's held charge supplies anticipation without inserting another input delay after release; the low catch and rise supply the follow-through. [Animation timing and follow-through](https://www.avgguild.org/post/12-principles-of-animation)
- Video references located for later visual review: [Jason Gardener sprint start, described as 500 fps](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UvPuT4nXnY8) and [Asafa Powell slow-motion start](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SebLUQFWFz0). Their search descriptions were available; the videos themselves were not viewed or analyzed frame by frame in this environment.
- The powered motor is now labeled `drive` for the whole powered thrust window, including after the initial pulse. Once thrust ends, arcade resistance and braking dissipate speed as the feet catch; this is an intentionally stylized interpretation rather than measured athlete physics.


## Near-ground skim and stabilization

The power-drive silhouette now lifts both soles a few render pixels above the ground, easing back into a planted catch; collision, gravity, attack sweeps and the dash motor still use the grounded plane. Both normal and power endings settle through a damped, flexing joint response before locomotion resumes. A jump pressed during the settle is queued; attacks remain available. The foot-level charge ring and gauge were removed; the charge still has body tremor, dust, camera feedback and an audio-ready cue. Arms are kept bent in a forward/neutral guard rather than locked behind the torso.
