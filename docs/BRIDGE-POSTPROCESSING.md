# Bridge post-processing: bounded blur, glow, and grade

This documents the current Bridge finish on Canvas 2D, WebGL2 Optimized, and WebGL2 Master. Older v3/v4 galleries and measurements remain historical; this is the graph now used by the live renderer.

## Per-tier graph

| Path | Bloom / blur | Gloom and grade | Motion treatment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Master · WebGL2** | Selective linear-HDR emitter bloom. Three downsample scales at per-axis divisors **4, 8, 16**, followed by one separate **1/4-size** composition target. Four small fullscreen filter draws total; no blur upsample chain. | A single final grade pass adds the bloom composite, applies the existing ACES fit and 32³ district LUT, and folds in the restrained edge falloff and cool/warm split tone. | A bounded, cached local shutter streak for eligible Bridge trails, plus explicit HDR strike emitters. No whole-screen motion smear. |
| **Optimized · WebGL2** | **No bloom targets or bloom filter draws.** No full-scene capture or replacement blur pass. | The same final-pass shape with a 16³ district LUT and subtle edge falloff; local fixture lenses remain available. | Cached local streaks; reduced motion falls back to a crisp cue. |
| **Canvas 2D fallback** | No scene-copy blur, `CanvasRenderingContext2D.filter`, or per-pixel post shader. | One cached **448×88** lens atlas (157,696 RGBA bytes while resident) supplies a single bounded gloom blit and a small, capped number of visible fixture lenses. Authored wetness and lighting stay in their existing route painters. | Atlas-backed local streaks, not captures of previous screen frames; the same small effect surface is reused. |

The bloom prefilter rejects display-white diffuse paint and admits only authored HDR emission; saturated strikes retain their color. Tight, medium, and wide skirts are combined in one no-feedback pass with three texture taps. The grade and gloom share the final grade shader, not an additional fullscreen shader. Literal Ronin black ink is protected by the existing bounded ink test. The HUD is a separate layer and is composed after the world; it is never a bloom, blur, or grade input.

### Allocation and fallback policy

The Master bloom targets are `[4, 8, 16, 4]` per-axis divisors: three actual scales plus a distinct 1/4 composite target. At a 1250×700 backing they total an estimated **507,368 bytes** in packed `R11F_G11F_B10F`, or **1,014,736 bytes** in `RGBA16F`. These are texture-allocation estimates, not measured GPU memory use or a performance promise. At the compact QA backing of 212×130, the bloom targets occupied **16,332 bytes** in packed format.

Packed bloom is admitted only when `EXT_color_buffer_float` is present **and** a real framebuffer-completeness probe succeeds. Otherwise Master uses renderable `RGBA16F` bloom targets when available; if its required HDR render target is not supported, the existing renderer admission/fallback policy applies. The graph does not mix formats recursively or silently degrade the whole Master scene to RGBA8.

The cached Canvas atlas is drawn only where the relevant local effects belong. The Master bloom and both GPU grades stay inside the existing world output. Neither path adds work to the combat simulation, changes authored glazing/cover, or grades the HUD.

## Verification evidence and limits

The current-source production build generated static pages successfully and was copied into an isolated standalone review stage; the staged server returned the page successfully. The direct compact Master QA against that staged build verified the three-scale graph, packed FBO format, radiometric fixtures, motion-preference behavior, weather, and combat deltas. It reported **3 bloom levels**, **4 filter draws** (three reductions plus the composite), **0 upsample passes**, 16,332 bloom-target bytes, no GL error, and zero tracked surfaces after teardown. The compact Master backing was 212×130, so these are renderer-correctness checks, not native-resolution device benchmarks.

The matched probes retained a measurable strike delta (183 of 302 sampled combat pixels changed; max HDR difference 1.68), a wet-road delta (230 of 254 samples changed), and **zero** wetness change on the sheltered road (66 samples); the open road changed 114 of 115 sampled pixels. The bloom shader fixture checked the knee, saturated arc, constant preservation, and independent tight/mid/wide weights. A local test line produced one cached streak blit with regular motion and none with the reduced-motion setting. The optimized road QA reported zero bloom levels and a null bloom receipt; context-loss QA exercised Canvas fallback and Master restoration while preserving the HUD and route composition.

Twenty `paint(0, false)` samples also record median, p95, and max **JavaScript/driver submission time**. They intentionally do not wait for GPU completion, compositor presentation, or scanout. Values from a headless browser are diagnostics only; they are not a physical-device FPS or universal-speedup claim. Packed-format support and performance still need representative physical hardware/browser validation.

**Opening-flow verification and limits:** the deterministic optics probes use `--direct`, which bypasses the menu and is not opening-flow evidence. Normal UI preparation was separately exercised in HeadlessChrome 153 with real **ENGAGE** and **RETRY** clicks, the glazing-atlas response held, and a deliberate **190 ms** main-thread hitch after three complete world/HUD submits. Reduced-motion and normal-motion runs both passed: the loading matte stayed pixel-black through the hitch, the first exposed screenshot contained world and HUD pixels, and teardown reported zero retained surfaces. The normal-motion run also sampled the actual `sr-cut-out` animation on animation frames: each 330 ms cut advanced through 21 samples and 17 distinct transforms before input opened. A follow-up `--cancel` run canceled a submitted Retry back to the title without exposing the pending frame. `.qa/slasher-reveal-frames.mjs --title-ready` independently captured the first exposed 1000×560 frames and counted **15 compositor `Display::DrawAndSwap` events** during each Engage and Retry cut. The touch run at 400×760 also passed. All these normal-policy opening runs correctly selected **Canvas 2D** (`mobile-canvas`), because HeadlessChrome identified its adapter as software; they do not validate WebGL startup.

A forced-diagnostic Master trace initially looked like a fence deadlock, but longer instrumentation corrected that diagnosis: even with the finish canvas visible above the matte, each compact Master fence eventually changed from `UNSIGNALED` / `TIMEOUT_EXPIRED` to `SIGNALED` / `ALREADY_SIGNALED` after roughly **125–145 ms**. The opening pace gate requires two consecutive submit intervals no longer than **100 ms**, so software-rendered Master never accumulated qualifying intervals. A separate forced compact Mobile-WebGL fixture also missed its 45-second `playing` wait and returned to `attract` in this software-browser setup. Do not weaken or bypass the readiness gate: forcing WebGL on SwiftShader is not a hardware opening test. The WebGL UI-opening path still needs validation on representative physical hardware; no hardware-wide speed claim is made.

The relevant checks are `npm run check:bridge`, `.qa/slasher-complete-opening.mjs --compact` and `--compact --animated --cancel` for normal UI Engage/Retry, `.qa/slasher-reveal-frames.mjs --title-ready` for first-exposure screenshots and compositor swaps, `.qa/slasher-ultra-finish.mjs --compact --direct --verify-optics --verify-bounds --verify-motion` for deterministic finish probes, and `.qa/slasher-bridge-finish.mjs --v4 --context-only --direct --compact` / `--v4 --road-only --direct` against the exact served build.
