**Serves:** S1 ("procedural, not a filter") · Rule 9 · REQ-706 (asset-free) · `19`.

The ask: make the game look *better* through **natural animation generated by code**,
not imported art. This document is the plan for that, and it starts with what the

**Three gaps, precisely:**
1. **No spring/Verlet secondary motion.** `grep -rli verlet` → none; `damp()` cannot
   overshoot, and secondary motion *is* overshoot. No cape/sash/scabbard/tassel lag.
2. **No motor-control kinematics.** Curves are smooth (Hermite, C1) but not
   *human-shaped*: no bell velocity profile, no speed–curvature coupling.
3. **No aperiodic micro-life.** Breathing is a looping clip, so a stationary player
   repeats an identical 4-second loop — the classic "dead puppet" tell.

That is the whole job. No assets, no new canvas memory, no GPU.

## 1. The three laws worth stealing from motor control

### 1.1 Minimum-jerk reaches (Flash & Hogan, 1985)
Human point-to-point movement is **approximately straight with a bell-shaped
tangential velocity profile**, and is reproduced by minimising the integral of
squared jerk — the solution being a **quintic in time**
(`https://www.jneurosci.org/content/jneuro/5/7/1688.full.pdf`).

```
τ = t / T                                  normalised time, 0..1
s(τ) = 10τ³ − 15τ⁴ + 6τ⁵                    position fraction along the path
v(τ) = 30τ² − 60τ³ + 30τ⁴                   speed fraction of peak
peak/mean = 1.875  ⇒  mean/peak = 0.533     the machine-checkable signature
```
Cost: five multiplies per moving target. **This replaces `easeInOut` on every reach,
draw, turn and lunge** — because a smoothstep is a *feel*, while this is a *measured
law of human movement*, and the difference is visible when a limb starts and stops.

Testable: `M-VELPROF` = mean/peak tangential speed over a strike; target **0.50–0.58**.
A linear interpolation scores 1.0. An ease-in-out scores ~0.6–0.7. Only a real bell
scores 0.53.

### 1.2 The two-thirds power law (Lacquaniti, Terzuolo & Viviani, 1983)
Angular velocity is proportional to curvature to the power **2/3** —
`A = k·C^(2/3)`, equivalently `V = k·r^(1/3)`: **the hand slows where the path bends
and speeds up where it straightens.**

Why this matters to a sword game: a blade arc parameterised by *uniform arc length*
is physically wrong and reads as weightless, precisely the failure a 2026 analysis
names — "sample it at a uniform parameter step and the cursor runs at roughly constant
arc-speed, which has no bell-shaped velocity profile and violates the two-thirds power
law at every bend" (`https://blog.crawlex.net/blog/mouse-path-fitts-law/`).

```
sample the arc by arc length with a curvature-coupled clock:
  dθ/ds = C(s)                     curvature along the arc
  speed(s) = k · r(s)^(1/3) = k · C(s)^(-1/3)
  ⇒ integrate s from 0 to L with dt_s = ds / speed(s)
```
Cost: precomputed once per *clip* (the arc shape is authored), stored as a lookup of
`N=32` arc-length samples. Zero per-frame cost. Testable: `M-POWER` = log–log slope
of angular speed vs curvature over a swing, target **0.60–0.75**.

### 1.3 Why not just add more keyframes
Because the perception is dominated by *velocity shape* and *overshoot*, not by
sample density. A 12-key linear strike looks worse than a 5-key minimum-jerk strike at
any frame rate. This is also why §2 (physics lag) beats §1 (more keys) for the
"living" quality: lag is what makes a body look like it has mass.

## 2. Secondary motion: the thing `damp()` cannot do

A spring chain = root point attached to the body + N points, each pulled to its rest
position relative to its parent, plus gravity, plus damping. "This runs in under 10
lines of update code per chain. A character with a ponytail (5 points), two earrings
(2 points each), and a cape (8 points) adds **17 point updates per frame**, which is
negligible CPU cost for significant visual improvement"
(`https://abratabia.com/game-animation/procedural-animation.php`).

### 2.1 What we hang the chains on (all drawn, all existing silhouette)
| Chain | Points | Stiffness | Damping ζ | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sword scabbard / saya | 4 | high | 0.7 (near-critical) | rigid object, small lag |
| sash / obi tail | 6 | medium | 0.35 | the readable cloth beat |
| sleeve cuff | 3 per arm | medium | 0.45 | sells arm speed |
| tassel / charm | 3 | low | 0.25 | fastest, smallest, most "alive" |
| topknot / hair | 5 | medium | 0.4 | reacts to head turns |

17–21 simulated points total. No new art, no new canvas: the chains are *strokes we
already draw*, with their vertices moved.

### 2.2 Parameters, and the exact failure each one causes
Taken from the SFM jigglebone troubleshooting table (a mature tool with the same
technique), which we adopt as our tuning guide **and** as unit-test cases
(`https://asfmcompile.com/sfm-jigglebones/`):

| Symptom | Cause | Our test |
|---|---|---|
| hair jitters/vibrates | stiffness high + damping very low | `M-SECONDARY`: no limit cycle; ζ ≥ 0.2 |
| cape swings forever | damping too low | settle to ±2 % within **0.6 s** |
| hair sinks into head | too much tip mass, no constraint | capsule push-out; penetration = 0 |
| skirt passes through legs | rest pose too close to body | min rest distance ≥ 1.2 × radius |
| nothing moves | chain not attached | assert non-zero velocity after a turn |

### 2.3 Integration and determinism
Semi-implicit Euler at the **fixed 60 Hz sim step** (never at render rate), with the
previous position stored for render interpolation (`19` §2):

```
v += (target - p) * k / m * dt - v * c / m * dt + gravity * dt
p += v * dt
for (i in 0..2) solveConstraints()     // segment length, capsule push-out, angle limit
```
Critical damping `c = 2·√(k·m)` is our upper bound; ζ ∈ [0.2, 0.8] by chain.
Verlet is the alternative: "a 20-point rope with 5 constraint iterations requires 100
distance constraint calculations per frame. Ten ropes require 1,000" — and for web
games the guidance is **10 points per rope, 3 iterations, skip off-screen objects**
(same source). We use Euler because we need velocities for the render interpolation.

**Determinism requirement:** chains advance only on sim ticks, with fixed `dt` and no
`Math.random()`. Wind comes from the profile's deterministic noise field, so replaying
a seed replays the whole motion. This keeps Rule 10 satisfiable and makes the 244-test
suite sufficient protection.

### 2.4 Wind and weather coupling (0 extra systems)
`07` already defines `windPx` per archetype. Reuse it: the same scalar that drifts the
clouds also drives chain gravity vector tilt and the sway amplitude. One number, two
layers responding — that is what makes a scene feel like *one place* rather than
separate effects. Rain adds `windY`, storm adds gust noise from the weather state
machine (`07` §8).

## 3. Micro-life: killing the loop tell

A looping idle reads as dead because the eye detects exact repetition in 2–4 cycles.
Three cheap fixes, all aperiodic, all deterministic from the run seed:

| Layer | Signal | Range | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Breath | 0.22–0.30 Hz, amplitude modulated by noise | chest ±1.5 %, shoulders ±0.8 % | 1 sine + 1 noise eval |
| Weight shift | random walk on hip offset, clamped | ±2 px, ≥1.5 s per target | 1 noise eval |
| Head micro-corrections | **saccade-like**: hold, then a fast 60–90 ms correction, not a lerp | ±2° with 1–3 s holds | 1 timer |
| Guard micro-tension | noise-modulated arm angle | ±1.2° | 1 noise eval |

The saccade detail is the one people feel without seeing: human gaze **jumps and
holds**, it does not ease. An eased head turn at any frame rate reads as a puppet.

`M-MICRO` measures idle joint RMS (target 0.4–2.5 px equivalent — non-zero, but
below the visible-jitter threshold) and `M-DEAD` counts consecutive frames with zero
joint delta while idle (target 0 except intentional holds and `stepRate > 0`).

## 4. Contacts: where natural motion becomes *graphics*

The highest-value use of procedural motion is not the body — it is the world reacting
to the body, because it costs nothing to draw and it sells the frame:

| Event | Response | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| footfall | dust puff + a 1-frame mesh squash + a shadow pop | 1–2 blits (already have a dust pool) |
| landing | compression overshoot on hips/knees (spring, ζ 0.5) then settle | 0 |
| dash start | ground smear + cloth chain impulse | 1 blit |
| blade contact | 2-frame vibrato on the blade chain (restores in 120 ms) | 0 |
| cloth in rain | chain mass + damping raised, windY added | 0 |

Sub-frame timing matters here: a footfall that lands between sim ticks must spawn at
the interpolated position (`19` §3.2), otherwise contact FX visibly lag the foot at
120 fps — the most common "why does this look cheap" artefact in browser games.

motion is the cheapest "higher graphics" available to this project by two orders of
magnitude** — which is why it should not wait behind the sky.

## 8. What makes this "higher graphics" rather than "more motion"

Ranked by perceived fidelity per unit of work, with the mechanism named:

1. **Overshoot and settle** (secondary chains) — mass. Without it a body is a puppet
   no matter how many keys it has.
2. **Bell velocity + curvature coupling** — intent. The eye identifies *how* a limb
   starts and stops as biological or mechanical within a few frames.
3. **Aperiodic life** — aliveness. Loops are the only thing that reads as "game asset".
4. **Contact response** — groundedness. Dust and squash connect the figure to the deck.
5. **Sub-frame accuracy** — polish. Contacts landing on the wrong frame is the tell
   that survives the highest frame rate.

None of the five requires an asset, a texture, a canvas or a byte of GPU memory. All
five are deterministic, seed-varied, and testable — which is exactly what the mandate
asked for when it said procedural, distinctive, and never a filter.

## 9. Open items

1. **`M-VELPROF` needs a per-clip exception list.** Some authored beats are *thrusts*
   and legitimately have a plateau; the test must assert "bell or documented plateau",
   not a single number. The registry goes in `tests/` with the clip name as the key.
2. **Chain count vs silhouette.** Adding 5 chains can muddy the stickman read at `low`
   detail. `03`'s salience law says one hero; the chains are ≤4 px strokes and must
   pass the FID-6 contrast probe, so `low` may keep only the sash.
3. **Determinism of `damp()`→spring conversions.** Existing `damp()` calls that
   become springs change trajectories and *may* shift a combat test. Those conversions
   are separate commits with the test name quoted, per D4.
4. **Saccade timing is a design guess** (60–90 ms holds) from general gaze behaviour,
   not from a cited study. Labelled `[J]`; the eye gate (D5) decides.
