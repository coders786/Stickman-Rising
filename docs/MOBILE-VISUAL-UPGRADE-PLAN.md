# Mobile visual upgrade plan

**Decision: make the base picture better before drawing more per frame.** Keep Canvas 2D, author for **Low at 1×**, and spend live work on combat, grounding and a few local accents. Treat every optional effect as a replacement or a budgeted addition, not a reason to turn up device pixel ratio (DPR).

**Status: bridge implemented; dry station in the optional preview build.** See [the playtest build and matched results](MOBILE-VISUAL-UPGRADE-RESULTS.md). The [engine map](GRAPHICS-ENGINE-MAP.md) describes the frame pipeline; the [tier contract](lighting/graphics-tiers.md) records existing quality behaviors. The targets below remain release gates, not results from physical phones.

## The five Ws

| W | Decision |
|---|---|
| **What** | A more cinematic 2.5D picture: readable fighters, deeper perspective, materials that read at phone size, and lights that feel attached to the environment. First deliver a matched A/B of one bridge stretch at Low 1× with no new runtime draw pass or backing store. Then prove the same rules in a dry station scene. |
| **Why** | Better composition, light and materials should do more work than extra particles, higher resolution or full-frame effects. Keep fast responses and consistent frame pacing in wet crowds, not just a good FPS number on an empty cold scene. |
| **Who** | Design first for players on the weakest phone we intend to support. **Art** owns the Low reference, palette and plates. **Combat/design** signs off on telegraphs, silhouette, timing and touch feel. **Rendering** owns draw/backing budgets, lifecycle and instrumentation. **Device QA** owns paired Android/iPhone results and can block rollout. A solo maintainer can fill all four roles; the sign-offs still stay separate. |
| **Where** | Start around the bridge advisory sign and streetlight. Change the bake recipes in `src/game/world/{art-grade,background,buildings}.ts` and `src/game/stage/viaduct.ts`, not combat. Run experiments in `src/game/render/{renderer,graphics,quality}.ts` and the background reflection path. Validate through the production game and `.qa/slasher-*`, on physical Android Chrome and iPhone Safari as well as desktop. |
| **When** | Baseline first; bridge art slice next; mobile bottleneck experiments after a measured baseline; reusable scene data after the art slice passes; dry-scene proof and device sign-off before the new rules become the default. No calendar or device commitments until the team can name the minimum phones and schedule access. |

## Why these techniques

| What the research supports | What we will do with it |
|---|---|
| MDN suggests pre-rendering repeating Canvas work, batching calls, reducing state changes and avoiding live `shadowBlur` where possible. [1](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Canvas_API/Tutorial/Optimizing_canvas) | Replace art inside existing plates. Use the same sprites, small masks and bounded batches for optional effects. Do not add a separate canvas layer or worker just because the API exists. |
| web.dev separates JavaScript work from painting and compositing; at 60 Hz the entire frame interval is about 16.7 ms, including browser overhead. [1](https://web.dev/articles/rendering-performance) | Reserve headroom. Measure how long game work takes **and** how often a new frame is ready. A short Canvas function call is not proof of a cheap frame. |
| The National Gallery describes depth through tonal gradation across planes; Filament documents how rougher surfaces spread highlights and smooth surfaces reflect more sharply. [1](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/glossary/aerial-perspective) [2](https://google.github.io/filament/Materials.md.html) | Paint depth and material differences into 2D art. Adopt the visual cues, not the cost of a live lighting/material simulation. |
| Riot’s VALORANT team keeps gameplay-impacting features across tiers and describes replacing a large translucent aim effect with a thin outline for clarity and performance. [2](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/valorant-shaders-and-gameplay-clarity) | A telegraph's readable shape and timing are required; spark trails and glow are not. Use fewer, clearer marks rather than a coat of translucent particles. |
| An Android game case study documents large changes in performance as a phone heats during play. It uses native ADPF, not a browser API. [2](https://developer.android.com/stories/games/lineagew-adpf) | Test sustained runs on devices. Keep our browser-based governor; do not assume native thermal access or copy the study's FPS numbers. |
| Long main-thread tasks can delay feedback; yielding between short chunks makes room for user-facing work. [1](https://web.dev/articles/optimize-long-tasks) | Keep invariant art out of run preparation. Split any measured, unavoidable warmup into bounded work units with cancellation and a complete first frame. |

## The visual target, beyond the five Ws

**Cinematic silhouettes, tactile surfaces, restrained light.** A player should see the depth of the scene at idle, the danger during anticipation, and the contact on impact. The world can look elaborate while the space behind the fighter stays simple.

| Layer / moment | Wet bridge: first slice | Dry station: second-scene proof | Shared rule and cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wide view / depth** | Warm air still hangs over dark tower masses. Reduce tiny window contrast in the area behind weapons and heads. The advisory sign and lamp break up the skyline without brightening the whole stage. | Muted cool arches recede in broad silhouettes; one warm far signal, away from the action band, establishes the setting. | Far edges and tone changes are soft in the plate. Midground shows more shape; the play plane has the sharpest useful edges. Use existing parallax, no live depth-of-field pass. |
| **Near view / light** | The streetlight, small keyed rim on nearby hardware and steady patch in the wet road share one origin. Keep the existing warm base and hue; add contrast locally, not with a global exposure change. | A roof fixture makes one diffuse patch on the platform and one facing edge on its enamel case. No mirror on dry tile; track recess detail stays behind the fight plane. | One dominant key and a few identifiable fixtures. Each fixture record anchors body, light, occluder and receiver. Low reads the body and baked spill. Optional shafts/glints stay on those same anchors. |
| **Surfaces / close-up** | A narrow light edge on the steel railing, dark joins where uprights meet concrete, broad dark asphalt with broken highlights only in wet areas. | A dull floor, sharp metal platform edge and soft dust in the joints. The same dark matte figure reads against a different floor color. | Difference of highlight shape before more texture. Bake wear, bevels and static contact into existing art. Actors keep planted foot shadows; only upgrade finish inside their shapes. No extra fill for microdetail. |
| **Movement / combat** | During a dash the deck and sign stay planted as distant buildings drift. During a strike the existing attack silhouette and hit cue win over rain. | The same dash and strike remain readable without dramatic weather. A small ambient loop can establish a living station, but must not look like a warning. | Review idle → windup → contact → recovery at real speed. Use pose, value, shape and timing, not color alone. No changes to hit timing, reach, cue envelope, or the fixed 1/60 s combat step. |
| **Screen / comfort** | No new full-screen flash or fog over the fight. Look near the screen edge during a roll; reflections never climb off the deck. | No screen treatment needed to make the platform readable. Preserve touch controls and safe areas in portrait and landscape. | HUD remains outside optics at its current device scale. Reduce shake and ornamental motion without deleting cues. Check combined lightning/combat sequences against W3C flash guidance, not just individual assets. [2](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Guides/Media_queries/Using_for_accessibility) [1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG20/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold) |

**Reference pack for each scene:** same-seed Low 1× before/after stills (idle, windup, contact), a short pan plus wet/dry crowd clip, and a grayscale readability sheet at phone size. Review normal-speed motion, including the narrow phone crop and camera ends. For a tier step, the lit floor, shoe contact, player readability, danger shape and HUD stay. Only secondary finish changes. The bridge palette is not a rule for the station.

## How and when to implement

| Milestone | Work and exit condition |
|---|---|
| **0 · Freeze the incumbent** | Capture the current working tree, including the prior lighting and tier work, as the A/B control; record revision, patch and fixture settings. Save seed-42 captures at locked Low and Medium 1×, plus a run with the normal adaptive default on the floor phone. Gather wet-crowd, idle, moving-camera and Engage/Retry baselines. Use `frameLog.ts` for cadence and collect raw work samples in the test harness; the existing smoothed JS readout is not a percentile. Choose minimum Android and iPhone models/OS/browser versions. Headless runs cover deterministic parity and lifecycle only. |
| **1 · Upgrade the bridge art, not the loop** | In the existing plates, tune foreground/mid/far contrast behind the action, then bake the three surface treatments in the table. Keep fixture coordinates, steady pools, road mask/collision and `BRIDGE_ART` identity. No new steady-state pass, extra backing, larger plate, scene-scale change or new post-processing. Approve the Low 1× pack plus fixed-state HUD/combat parity and phone budget before starting more polish. |
| **2 · Buy headroom where the trace points** | First candidate: on Medium, compare current capture with the existing local forward actor mirrors and baked spill at fixed 1×. In [one headless portrait diagnostic](GRAPHICS-ENGINE-MAP.md#2-where-to-spend-and-what-to-measure-first), the same-frame road sample charged appreciable time to its hook; work can move between Canvas hooks and the two routes don't look identical. A/B **whole frames** on phones; keep capture if local reflections lose the visual read or don't improve the sustained tail. If cost instead follows visible bodies, profile repeated pose/path work and allocations first. If it follows resolution or blended area, clip and reduce area first. If the issue is startup, pre-bake invariant detail, then split the measured city/road jobs; don't fix a slow Retry by lowering play quality. |
| **3 · Make the rules reusable** | Extract a shared `StageLayout` (flat combat plane, spawn/camera limits, receiver regions) from bridge-specific constants and a `SceneArt` recipe (palette roles, depth bands, material/fixture records and optional weather). Keep view/pass order, HUD, combat clocks, quality manager and surface ownership shared. First prove the bridge is unchanged by this extraction in fixed replays. Then author the dry station on the same flat layout interface. No second renderer or unconditional rainy-bridge pass. |
| **4 · Roll out with a safe fallback** | Keep route/effect experiments behind a test switch until verified. At locked settings pass the paired gates below; then test with normal adaptation on, including Medium→Low→Medium. Preserve the user's saved ceiling and the HUD scale. Shed decorative work before jeopardizing cues, and use the current ladder's dwell rather than allocating/resizing on every slow frame. If an optional feature fails, disable or replace just that feature; retain approved base art and the incumbent route. |

### Work budgets a new scene must declare

- **Required:** one opaque base, its existing depth and arena layers, poses/contact, threats/telegraphs and HUD. Low requires **zero new viewport-sized stores, full-frame captures, filters or steady-state passes** for the art upgrade. Bakes/atlases must be accounted for at decoded size and released by their scene owner. More art detail still has to pass startup, memory and cadence checks.
- **Optional:** a recipe names the emitter/receiver, max visible instances, max draws/fills, clipped destination area, owning surface scope and fallback. Cull before doing optional geometry. As a **starter cap for new mobile accents**, use at most two local sprite blits **total per frame**, summed clipped area no more than 10% of the world backing, and reuse existing tiles. If the cap is exceeded, admit the most relevant visible light or draw only its baked spill; do not spend this budget per new fixture. Any exception is a separately measured opt-in, not a silent raise of the cap.
- **Pixels:** `backing area ≈ CSS width × height × scene DPR²`. At 390×844 CSS, 1× is **329,160 pixels / ~1.26 MiB raw RGBA** for one canvas; 2× is **4× the pixels / ~5.02 MiB**, before the HUD, transient stores or browser overhead. An 80×140 CSS clipped stamp covers ~3.4% of that view, whereas a full-frame blend covers 100%. Count overlapping draws separately. This is a work proxy, not elapsed time or GPU memory. Measure scale separately from effect count.

## Performance and visual release gates

1. **Pair like with like.** On a minimum Android (Chrome), a representative midrange Android, and an iPhone at the intended Safari floor, run the production build in portrait and landscape. Log device/OS/browser, actual refresh offer, scene/HUD backing, effective tier and route, seed/input, brightness and power conditions. First use the same fixed scale and tier on A and B so a hidden downgrade cannot win the test. Use scripted matched frames for pixels, and real-time input for pacing. Run short A/A repeats to establish noise, then at least three alternated A/B samples for the affected fixture. For final sign-off, play each version for 15 minutes and compare the last few minutes of the dry and wet-crowd workload, in addition to cold launch. Repeat close results; do not widen gates to fit variance.
2. **Frame time, not average FPS.** For a 60 Hz configuration, proposed targets are **≤8 ms p95** in main-thread gameplay work (simulation plus Canvas calls together), and foreground frame intervals **≤18 ms p95 / ≤25 ms p99**. At equal tier, scale and fixture, use the median of paired deltas and allow at most **+0.5 ms JS p95**, **+1 ms frame p99** and **+0.5 percentage points** in intervals over 1.5× the actual refresh interval versus the incumbent. Require no new repeatable renderer-attributed >50 ms gameplay stall or added dropped simulation debt over the paired run. If noise makes a threshold inconclusive, hold the decision. If the incumbent already misses the absolute target on a phone, require non-regression for the art slice, keep the optional upgrade off by default, and do not claim a sustained 60 FPS mode for that device. Do not work around a failed gate with an untested device allowlist.
3. **Know which time was measured.** Log raw gameplay work samples, cadence/long gaps, cap admissions and dropped debt separately. `rAF − JS` is **not** GPU time, and cadence alone is not presented FPS. Use short diagnostic traces separately from low-overhead pacing runs without tracing, screenshots or pixel reads. Chrome DevTools distinguishes timely, partial and dropped frames; use remote Android debugging with screencast off. On iPhone use Web Inspector Timelines to diagnose JS, rendering and memory, and don't infer actual screen presentation from a JS counter. [1](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/devtools/performance/reference) [1](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/devtools/remote-debugging) [1](https://webkit.org/web-inspector/enabling-web-inspector/) [1](https://developer.apple.com/safari/tools/)
4. **Count stutters outside combat.** Measure Engage→visible acknowledgement and Engage→playable separately, fresh and Retry. Avoid a new >50 ms foreground task; for new prep scheduling aim for ≤8 ms main-thread chunks with a yield between them. Track peak + settled ledger bytes, no growth through 20 Retry/resize cycles and zero owned stores on teardown. Exercise portrait rotation, cancellation, context-loss fallback, pause/background/resume and governor shed/recovery. Do not loop allocating art or reroll the scene on a tier step. Existing startup costs are not cleared merely by moving a task off the gameplay frame.
5. **Approve the picture and the fight.** Compare equivalent action and pose, with exact combat events and HUD for renderer-only changes. Check sampled tap-to-visible-response on-device too. Require the intended image differences in the art slice, not byte-identical scenery. A route replacement must retain plausible light/floor alignment and stable motion at the same view even though pixels differ. Reject a build that hides a windup, makes a dry floor glossy, flattens the scene at Low or loses touch feedback; saving time does not excuse a visual or gameplay regression.

**Repo checks to reuse:** [slasher replay](../.qa/slasher-replay.mjs) (`--fixed --wet --fixture=crowd --tier=low --scale=1 --route=capture` and a forward control, `--pixels` only outside timing), [startup](../.qa/slasher-startup.mjs), [tier](../.qa/slasher-quality.mjs) and [gesture](../.qa/slasher-gestures.mjs) smoke (`--touch` for the latter two), [lifetime soak](../.qa/slasher-lifetimes.mts) and `npm run build:iso`. Run `npm run gate` and compare to the frozen control, which has five known combat failures in the [last tier receipt](lighting/graphics-tiers.md). No new failures, no relaxed combat assertions. Neither emulated touch nor software headless timing closes a physical phone gate.
